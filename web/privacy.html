Privacy

The short version: we designed point.vote so there is almost nothing to have a privacy policy about.

No accounts, no tracking

There is no sign-up, no login, and no user database. The site sets no cookies and runs no analytics, no ad code, and no third-party scripts — every asset is served from this domain. Your browser keeps a per-room participant token in session storage, which your browser deletes when the tab closes; it never identifies you as anything more than the name you typed into a room.

Rooms evaporate

Rooms live in memory on our server and are deleted roughly two hours after their last activity. There is no archive, no backup, and no way for us to recover a room after it expires. Your estimates are arguments, not records.

Votes are structurally secret until reveal

While a round is open, the server will not return any vote's value to anyone — not to other participants, not to the room's creator, and not in its own logs. This is enforced in the server's wire format, not by politeness. (We measured why this matters — see the anchoring experiment.)

What the server logs

Operational logs record request paths, status codes, timings, and room-level events (a room was created, a vote was cast, a round was revealed) — not IP addresses, not vote values attributed to participants, and not rationales. Client IP addresses are used transiently, in memory only, for rate limiting.

Infrastructure

Traffic reaches us through Cloudflare, which terminates TLS and sees traffic the way any CDN does; their privacy policy applies to that hop. The application itself runs on hardware we own.
